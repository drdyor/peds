# Design Direction

## Chosen approach: Clinical Notebook / Editorial Study Desk

A tactile, editorial study interface that feels like a carefully organized paediatrics revision notebook rather than a generic quiz app. The experience uses a warm paper ground, cobalt ink, coral annotations, and purposeful asymmetry so that reviewing cards feels calm, focused, and memorable.

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial stationery with Swiss-influenced information design and subtle screenprint texture.

### Core Principles
- Make the active card the clear focal point while keeping review status visible at a glance.
- Use paper, ink, and annotation cues to make progress feel tangible.
- Prefer structured asymmetry over a centered quiz template.
- Keep controls tactile, direct, and keyboard-friendly.

### Color Philosophy
Warm ivory creates a low-fatigue study surface. Cobalt carries the authority of medical ink and anchors navigation. Coral is reserved for difficulty and review decisions; butter yellow marks memory and momentum. The ownable signature color is **Cobalt Clinic #1647E8**.

### Layout Paradigm
A two-column study desk: a narrow left rail for the deck identity and progress, and a generous right workspace for the current card. On small screens, the rail becomes a compact top band. The card is offset rather than mathematically centered, with a slim metadata strip and a generous response area.

### Signature Elements
A small flashcard-star mark, underlined annotation rules, and capsule-like review counters that resemble labels in a clinical notebook.

### Interaction Philosophy
Actions should feel like marking a card by hand: one clear tap to reveal, one decisive tap to classify. Hard brings the card back into the active review queue; Easy moves it to the learned pile. Progress persists in the browser so the desk remembers the learner.

### Animation
Use short, quiet transitions: 160–220ms slide-and-fade between cards, gentle lift on hover, and no motion that competes with reading. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Use Fraunces for display headlines and section labels, paired with DM Sans for interface text and card content. Headlines are compact and slightly editorial; body copy stays open, readable, and high contrast.

### Brand Essence
A focused paediatrics revision desk for learners who want their study queue to feel organized and human. Personality: **precise, warm, quietly energetic**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and reassuring. CTAs sound like study actions, not marketing slogans.

> “One card at a time. Keep the signal.”
>
> “Mark it hard. We’ll bring it back.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a folded index card with a four-point spark, used as a small cobalt stamp beside the wordmark.
